# The Everything World: a walking guide

What each place should feel like before anything is built in Unity. This is a design draft to react to: change anything that feels wrong. The hard facts (names, palettes, landmarks) live in `build/scene_art.json` and `ART_PLAN.md`; this guide is the vibe on top.

**How to read an entry:** what you see as you arrive, what it feels like to be there (light, weather, sound), and what the artist areas there should look like. Numbers are songs, artists and land area.

## The whole world in one paragraph

About 15.5 × 10.4 km. One big continent in the middle (the Old Kingdoms joined to Riffland), three landmasses around it (the Groovelands to the west, the Indie Isles to the east, the Chart Isles further east), and island chains toward the edge (the Boroughs top-left, the Bass Reaches bottom-right, the Isles of Story in an inner sea). In the middle of that inner sea sits the Everything Hub. Each realm has one fast-travel tower. Every land is at least 0.44 km², big enough to wander in for a few minutes without passing an artist area.

**Where the music changes, you can see it (the owner's idea).** Wherever two lands meet there's a shimmering curtain: invisible from far away, fading in as you approach, and always see-through. It's tinted with the colour of the land you're about to enter, so the same border looks different from each side. Bridges have one at each end. The areas of artists with their own radio station have a warm gold one round them, exactly where the music switches to that artist. Each kind has its own pattern (land borders a soft shimmer, bridge gates horizontal bands, artist rings vertical stripes), and as you walk up to one, the name of what's beyond fades in on it, so overlapping curtains stay easy to tell apart.

**Built for a group of friends.** Every building is sized for several people at once, not one: doorways at least 2.8 m wide and 3.4 m tall, rooms people gather in at least 8 m across, 3 m aisles, and places to sit. Lands should feel spread out, with space between things. (The numbers live in `HumanScale.cs`.)

**Light comes from things in the world.** Dusk and night lands keep their ambient light low, so lanterns, windows, fires and candles actually light the ground around them, each with a soft glow.

**One idea that runs through everything: each land keeps its own sky.** Stepping from the Hair Metal Strip (neon night) into the Yacht Rock Riviera (endless sunset) changes the light, fog and weather over a few seconds, the way the music changes. The world has no shared day and night; each place is always at its best time of day. (Decided 2026-11-08.)

## The Everything Hub

*The whole playlist, 2,166 songs · a round island in the inner sea · tower: The Everything Mast*

**Arriving:** A broad round island alone in the inner sea, always in golden late-afternoon light. Long causeways reach it from the Prog Marches, the Indie Downs and Studio Lot Island, and each one becomes a paved path into the plaza. Round the whole hub, a great ring of album covers turns slowly; you pass under it as you arrive.

**The vibe:** A grand crossroads, and a trophy hall for the whole playlist. This is what all of us built together, song by song, and the hub is proud of it. The station plays anything from the whole playlist, so you never know what's next. It's the place to meet before heading out and to come back to afterwards. Every visit is an occasion, whatever day it is.

**What's here:** No artists, on purpose. Instead:
- **The ring of every cover.** Every song's album cover, big (over 3 m), in the order it was added, spiralling up round the whole hub from the first song to the newest, with the year marked where each new one begins. The lowest turn starts just over head height, so you can walk up under the first songs and look at them closely. Each person who adds songs has their own bright frame colour (the dais tablet shows whose is whose). The song playing now gets a white frame and a beam of light down to the ground beneath it. The ring turns once every 50 minutes and can be seen from across the water.
- **The dais.** A round stone platform at the foot of the mast, ringed in glowing bronze, with ramps on four sides. Standing tablets carry the playlist's numbers and the names of everyone who built it.
- **The stands.** Curved stone tiers all round the plaza, facing the mast, for sitting together under the ring. They open where the paths come in.
- **The jukebox.** A classic arched jukebox with rainbow light tubes round its top, showing the song on the radio, its cover, its artist and which land it belongs to. Beside it, a big wooden arrow on a post swings round to point at that land, with the land's name and distance written on it.
- **The signpost.** One arm for every realm's tower, with the distance.
- **The map board.** The whole world's map on a big standing board, north up, with a glowing "you are here" dot.
- **The park.** Past the stands, a formal park: rows of round trees and clipped dark cones, planted in rings. Between the rows, an avenue of white stone statues of music notation on plinths: treble and bass clefs, notes, a sharp, a flat, a fermata, a rest.
- **The sky.** Always golden late afternoon, but alive: clouds drift on the day's wind (some hours clear, some hours full), gulls circle the mast, and the light takes its colour from each song, bright amber for happy songs and rose and violet for sad ones. Every new song sends a golden rocket up from the dais, and some songs (more often the more energetic they are) get a fireworks show over the ring.
- **The quiet bench.** At the water's edge, away from the paths and the stands: one bench, one lantern, no sign.

## The Old Kingdoms (centre of the continent)

*Classic rock, jam bands, prog, southern rock, glam and heavy metal · tower: The Old Radio Tower*

The heartland. Old hills, long roads and the tallest mountains in the world. Most of it feels like a timeless afternoon, with darker metal country in the high peaks.

### The Jam Band Commonwealth
*116 songs · 23 artists · 0.62 km²*

**Arriving:** Rolling woods with long trails that loop back on themselves, opening onto meadows used as festival lawns. Above the trees a hot-air balloon is tethered over a forest amphitheatre.

**The vibe:** Golden late afternoon sliding into lantern light. Tie-dye tapestries strung between trees, tour vans parked under the oaks, campfires, someone's banjo on a cabin porch. Unhurried: the paths are long because the songs are long.

**Artist areas:** Phish (66 songs) is the biggest of the world's three temples: picture a festival ground of its own, with a stage, a lot full of vans and a long winding "jam path". Trey Anastasio and Widespread Panic get halls nearby; smaller artists get campsites and porch stages.

### Kingdom of Classic Rock
*86 songs · 21 artists · 0.93 km²*

**Arriving:** Old green hills worn smooth, two-lane highways, stone bridges. On the highest hill a crumbling stadium stands like a castle.

**The vibe:** Warm, timeless afternoon. Jukeboxes in lay-bys, vintage cars, standing stones shaped like records, and a brightly painted submarine on the lake. It should feel like a place everyone already knows.

**Artist areas:** The Beatles get a temple (20 songs); Led Zeppelin, Bowie, Hendrix and the Stones get halls built like old music halls and roadside monuments. Smaller artists get record-shaped standing stones along the roads.

### The Psychedelic Wilds
*58 songs · 30 artists · 0.56 km²*

**Arriving:** Hot desert mesas striped with swirling colour, melting into a forest of giant mushrooms. A spiral mushroom towers over everything.

**The vibe:** Heat haze, and a sky that slowly cycles through colours. Lava-lamp plants, painted camper vans, crystals. Slightly dreamy and slightly too hot.

**Artist areas:** Grateful Dead (17 songs) gets a temple: a desert stage under a mushroom canopy. Others are mushroom rings, painted vans and crystal shrines.

### The Folk Woods
*39 songs · 27 artists · 0.52 km²*

**Arriving:** Old-growth forest around a mountain lake, with a railway running along the shore. A huge old tree has a small stage built round its roots.

**The vibe:** A clear, gentle morning. Canoes, cabins, smoke from a campfire, a harmonica left on a stump. Birdsong and the occasional train.

**Artist areas:** Cabins, lakeside benches and stumps with plaques. Fenne Lily and Cat Stevens get the biggest cabins.

### The Yacht Rock Riviera
*38 songs · 23 artists · 0.50 km²*

**Arriving:** Palm-lined coves, white sand and a calm marina. The yacht club's pier is shaped like a piano keyboard.

**The vibe:** An endless late-summer sunset. Sailboats, deck chairs, white grand pianos on the sand. Smooth and slightly cheesy, on purpose.

**Artist areas:** Elton John gets a hall (a white piano pavilion). The rest get moored boats, beach cabanas and deck-chair plaques.

### The Prog Marches
*37 songs · 13 artists · 0.54 km²*

**Arriving:** A wide, strange marchland of roads that run in long odd loops. A giant prism on the plain splits a beam of light across the valley.

**The vibe:** Twilight with the stars already out. Observatories, giant clockwork, standing stones spaced in 7/8, old organs left in fields. Thoughtful and a little unsettling. It's also the shore closest to the Everything Hub.

**Artist areas:** Pink Floyd and Frank Zappa get halls (an observatory and a mad workshop). Others are clockwork plinths and organ-shrines.

### The Iron Range
*29 songs · 16 artists · 0.48 km²*

**Arriving:** The tallest peaks in the world, honeycombed with mines. A forge inside the highest peak pours a river of molten metal down the mountain.

**The vibe:** Storm clouds and forge glow. Chains, anvils, mine carts, rusted gates. Loud, heavy, dramatic, and the hardest climb in the world.

**Artist areas:** Metallica gets a hall carved into the rock like a mine entrance. Others are anvil plinths and iron-gated alcoves along the mine roads.

### The Southern Rock Bayou
*25 songs · 10 artists · 0.64 km²*

**Arriving:** Cypress swamp and slow brown rivers. A juke joint on stilts sits under a huge oak hung with Spanish moss.

**The vibe:** A humid sunset with fireflies. Rocking chairs, motorcycles, a slide guitar on the porch. Plenty of room to wander, with few artists.

**Artist areas:** Porches and stilt shacks over the water. Marshall Tucker Band, the Allman Brothers and CCR get the biggest porches.

### The Hair Metal Strip
*22 songs · 11 artists · 0.45 km²*

**Arriving:** A neon boulevard winding through a dry desert valley, leading to a giant club with pyro jets on its roof.

**The vibe:** Neon night. Leopard print, giant hairspray cans, motorcycles. Loud and silly in the best way.

**Artist areas:** Neon signs and club fronts along the boulevard; Guns N' Roses, Aerosmith and Mötley Crüe get the biggest marquees.

### The Bluegrass Hollows
*21 songs · 18 artists · 0.68 km² · new*

**Arriving:** Blue-green ridges and deep wooded hollows, creeks, split-rail fences. A barn with its doors open on a porch stage, and a picking circle that never stops.

**The vibe:** Quick-fingered and homespun. A covered bridge, a water mill, mason jars on the rail. Sits between the jam bands and country, which is exactly right.

**Artist areas:** Porches, rocking chairs and fiddle-shaped signposts. Lots of spare land here for the bluegrass you add later.

### Arena Rock Ridge
*16 songs · 11 artists · 0.44 km²*

**Arriving:** A long mountain ridge with a stadium carved into it, its spotlights sweeping the sky.

**The vibe:** Night under stadium lights. Lighters held up, tour buses, a cowbell somewhere. Big and earnest.

**Artist areas:** Tour buses and lit stage-wings along the ridge. Blue Öyster Cult gets the biggest one (with a cowbell, obviously).

## Riffland (north half of the continent)

*Alternative, grunge, punk, emo, metalcore, prog metal and J-rock · tower: The Amp Stack Spire*

Rougher, louder and more modern than the Old Kingdoms it's joined to. Weather turns: drizzle, ash, overcast moors, neon dusk.

### Metalcore Caldera
*85 songs · 48 artists · 1.09 km²*

**Arriving:** A ring of black volcanoes around a sunken crater, ash drifting down, lava cracks in the ground. A circular pit arena sits in the crater floor.

**The vibe:** Smoky, overcast, lit red from below. Toppled amp stacks, chain-link, obsidian spires, steam vents. The biggest land in the world, so there's room to roam the rim.

**Artist areas:** Killswitch Engage and Like Moths to Flames get halls (fortified amp-stack stages). The rest are obsidian shrines and chain-link pens round the rim.

### The Alt Rock Moors
*66 songs · 28 artists · 0.54 km²*

**Arriving:** Grey-green moorland, dry-stone walls, low hills, scattered small towns. A giant radio transmitter on the tallest hill blinks its warning lights.

**The vibe:** Moody overcast with sudden breaks of sun. Phone boxes, a lonely bus stop, power lines humming.

**Artist areas:** Radiohead gets a hall (a transmitter station on the moor). Others are bus shelters, phone boxes and stone huts with plaques.

### The Punk Badlands
*50 songs · 28 artists · 0.91 km²*

**Arriving:** Scrappy badlands with suburban ruins and skate bowls carved into the ground. A giant drained swimming pool has become a skatepark.

**The vibe:** Harsh, bright suburban noon. Graffiti, shopping carts, battered mailboxes. Now also home to the ska bands, so expect checkerboard patterns.

**Artist areas:** Green Day and Fall Out Boy get halls (a garage-turned-venue and a half-pipe arena). Others are graffiti walls, ramps and mailboxes.

### The Math Rock Escarpment
*43 songs · 33 artists · 0.52 km²*

**Arriving:** Sheer cliffs cut into geometric terraces stepping down to a calm sea, waterfalls everywhere. A lone wooden house sits on the cliff edge by the biggest fall.

**The vibe:** Crisp autumn with long shadows. Wind chimes, string lights, fallen leaves. Quiet between bursts of noise.

**Artist areas:** Small terraces each with a bench and wind chime; MONO and tricot get the larger terraces by the falls.

### The Grunge Rainwood
*40 songs · 20 artists · 0.88 km²*

**Arriving:** Dripping pine forest on steep slopes, muddy logging roads, an old sawmill turned venue next to a roaring waterfall.

**The vibe:** Constant drizzle. Flannel on clotheslines, rusted trailers, a coffee shack with one light on.

**Artist areas:** Trailers, cabins and sawmill sheds; Smashing Pumpkins and Soundgarden get the biggest.

### The Post-Punk Docklands
*39 songs · 25 artists · 0.52 km²*

**Arriving:** A grey industrial harbour of quays and brutalist blocks, cranes overhead. One tower block has a single lit window.

**The vibe:** A wet night under sodium lights. Flyers on walls, puddles, shipping containers.

**Artist areas:** Shipping containers and warehouse doors with posters. The Smiths get a hall in an old warehouse.

### The Djent Spires
*33 songs · 27 artists · 0.63 km²*

**Arriving:** Precise crystal spires and terraced cliffs with clean, fractal edges. A fractal citadel floats above the highest spire.

**The vibe:** Crisp, clear, cold light. Floating cubes, Tesla coils, bridges strung like guitar strings. Where the two halves of the continent meet.

**Artist areas:** Geometric monoliths and floating plinths. Leprous, Plini and Sithu Aye get the largest.

### The J-Rock Isles
*29 songs · 22 artists · 0.62 km²*

**Arriving:** A dense neon city on steep ground, softened by cherry trees. A red torii gate stands on a rooftop high above.

**The vibe:** Dramatic dusk with big clouds. Vending machines, train crossings, paper lanterns, tangled power lines.

**Artist areas:** Shopfronts and rooftop shrines with lanterns; Hoshikuma Minami gets the largest.

### The Oddball Backroads
*28 songs · 17 artists · 0.96 km²*

**Arriving:** Winding rural back roads that feel slightly wrong, overgrown with kudzu. A giant fibreglass head stands next to a crooked water tower.

**The vibe:** Hazy summer, slightly off. Lawn flamingos, junk sculptures, a thrift-store couch in a field. Lots of room to get pleasantly lost.

**Artist areas:** Roadside attractions, one per artist: Pixies and Ween get the strangest ones.

### The Nu Metal Quarry
*27 songs · 13 artists · 0.62 km²*

**Arriving:** A huge open-pit quarry with rusted machinery on its terraces and a stage built on a mining rig at the bottom.

**The vibe:** Overcast and gritty. Burning oil drums, chain-link, wrecked cars.

**Artist areas:** Nickelback gets a hall (a quarry rig, naturally). Others are machinery and truck beds on the terraces.

### The Power Metal Peaks
*20 songs · 10 artists · 0.57 km²*

**Arriving:** Fantasy mountains above a stormy pirate cove. A castle on a peak with a dragon coiled round it, a pirate ship moored beneath.

**The vibe:** An epic sunset. Banners, swords, treasure chests, and neon kawaii signs because half the artists here are Japanese idol-metal bands.

**Artist areas:** Banners and shrines on the mountain path; BRIDEAR gets the biggest.

## The Indie Isles (east landmass)

*Indie rock, shoegaze, bedroom pop and indie folk · tower: The Lo-Fi Lighthouse*

Softer and greener than the continent. Overcast skies, fairy lights, dusk. Close to the hub.

### The Indie Downs
*50 songs · 33 artists · 0.98 km² · built in Unity*

**Arriving:** Rolling chalk downs in faded, sun-bleached colour, like an old photo of a summer day. Golden late-summer grass in deep drifts, full of poppies, cornflowers and daisies; hedgerows, copses on the hilltops, sheep grazing, round hay bales in the fields, and a giant guitar cut into the white chalk of a hillside facing the hub's causeway. The light is low, warm and hazy, and seeds and pollen float through the air all round you, drifting faster when the wind is up: the Downs' own weather, the way snow is Lanternwood's.

**The vibe:** A late-summer afternoon you only half remember. Muted and changeable rather than sad. The weather follows the music more than on any other land: quiet songs cloud the sky over and the light goes soft and grey-blue; energetic songs bring the sun out warm, and the wind picks up, so the long grass and bunting race, the kites climb and the windmill's sails spin faster. Every new song starts with a gust. Some songs bring a surprise, the same for everyone there: a passing summer shower (likelier for quiet, sad songs) with a rainbow after it, a murmuration of starlings swirling over the hills, or a hot-air balloon drifting across the sky for the length of the song. Low clouds drift over, their shadows sweeping across the hills. Tall landmarks to find your way by: a white windmill, a flint church with a spire and a churchyard, a brick folly tower on a hilltop, and rows of tall poplars. At the heart is a village green: cottages in faded paint, a maypole with bunting, a red phone box, a postbox, bicycles and lamp posts covered in gig posters.

**Two paths:** Two chalk paths wind across the Downs, one through the artists added by one person and one through the artists added by another, each marked with glowing waymarker lanterns in that person's colour that pulse softly, and blaze while a song that person added is playing. Every area wears the colour of whoever added it. The paths cross at the artists both of them added.

**Artist areas:** Death Cab for Cutie: the record shop on a little high street, with a striped awning, record bins full of covers and a listening booth. Florence Road: an open-air stage on a lawn where the two paths meet, lit in both colours, with festoon lights and hay bales to sit on. Rainbow Kitten Surprise: a rainbow-painted bus stop on a lane to nowhere. Pavement: where the two paths meet, a bench, its covers and bunting in both colours. Everyone else gets a small summer moment: a picnic under a tree, a market stall, deckchairs and a windbreak, a viewpoint with a telescope, bikes against a fence, a phone box on a hill.

**A quiet place:** Away from the paths, a great tree on level ground with a ring of stone walkway round it, four carved standing stones, benches facing the tree, lanterns in its branches and low lights along the walk. No sign.

### Lanternwood
*~40 songs · 23 artists · 0.53 km² · built in Unity*

**Arriving:** Birch woods dusted with snow, open clearings, and a frozen creek running down into a frozen lake you can walk out on. An old chapel full of candles, big enough for a group, with pews facing the altar.

**The vibe:** Deep blue dusk under a sky full of stars, light only from lanterns, windows, candles and fires. Snowmen stand beside the paths (somewhere new each day). A fire circle with log seats to gather round, and a lone bench on high ground looking out to sea. Hushed.

**Artist areas:** Each hall is different. Phoebe Bridgers: a weathered boathouse on the lake shore with a moon window, and a long pier out over the ice ending in a bench (a place to sit alone). Big Thief: a treehouse on four birches, reached by a ramp, strung with lights, with a fire pit and log seats below. Smaller artists: lean-tos, lantern posts and benches in clearings, all joined by lantern-lit paths.

### The Shoegaze Fens
*42 songs · 33 artists · 0.76 km²*

**Arriving:** A reverb-drowned marsh of reeds, still pools and weeping willows. A ruined cathedral sinks into the marsh, glowing softly inside.

**The vibe:** Permanent soft fog and heavy bloom. Floating petals, old guitar pedals used as stepping stones. Everything slightly out of focus.

**Artist areas:** Small islands in the marsh, each with a glowing stone. Deafheaven and Mazzy Star get the biggest.

### The Driftwood Shore
*33 songs · 19 artists · 0.74 km² · built in Unity*

**Arriving:** The morning after a storm on a cold shore. Silver sea, silver sky, pearl mist, and a thin line of rose and gold where the light is trying to break. Low cloud races in off the sea on a strong wind, waves break on the sand, and salt mist blows across the dunes. You arrive at the heart of it: a still lagoon ringed by a low boardwalk, a little footbridge across it, and a curved arch of bleached driftwood rising out of the water over the bridge, lashed with rope, sea-glass floats and a ship's bell hanging from it and a lantern burning at each foot. Every artist is close round the lagoon; from there a long promenade with a signpost marked "the sea" runs out across the dunes to the beach.

**The vibe:** Bittersweet. The colours come from its covers: silver-grey and rose, ember, pencil-grey. The storm has left everything strewn along the tide line: bleached logs and branches, seaweed, shells, buoys, lost crab pots, sea glass glinting in aqua, green and amber, tide pools with starfish, and a wreck half buried in the sand with its broken mast still up. The warmth is in small things: bonfires still smouldering with a thermos left on a log, a row of faded beach huts with a couple of windows lit, lanterns along the low grey boardwalk (rope rails you can lean on), a lifeguard tower where the promenade meets the sand, the bones of a whale on the beach, and a beacon at the end of a jetty running straight out into open water, sweeping its beam through the mist. Beyond the boardwalk the dunes and heath are wild but never empty: wind-bent trees leaning away from the sea, sand fences half buried, cairns with a piece of sea glass glowing on top, fishing nets drying on poles, stacks of old crab pots, lone benches with a lantern facing the view, upturned boats made into sheds with a lit window, and a roofless stone cottage with a lamp still burning on the hearth. Silvery marram grass bends in the wind on the dunes; grey-green heath and pink sea thrift inland. The light breaks rose and gold for hopeful songs and turns cold silver for sad ones; some sad songs bring a passing drizzle. When "Somewhere Over the Rainbow" plays, a rainbow rises out toward the sea, seen through the driftwood arch, and stays while it plays. It is never quite the same twice: gulls always wheel over the shore and in some songs a big flock comes in off the sea; an old sailing ship sometimes crosses the horizon with a lantern lit at its stern; now and then a whale spouts three times out at sea and lifts its tail; lightning flickers far out over the water in the drizzly songs; in some hopeful songs the cloud breaks and shafts of light fall on the sea; and every day a message in a bottle washes up somewhere different, glowing faintly green. All of it is the same for friends standing together.

**Artist areas:** Jack Johnson: a grey shingled beach house on stilts with a deck, a hammock, a guitar by the door and a fire pit below; string lights swagged round the deck, surfboards leaning by the door, a telescope pointed at the sky ("Constellations"), a rain barrel ("Monsoon"), towels on a washing line and an outdoor shower. Agnes Obel: a black grand piano standing in a pool of still water, candles burning on it and a ring of candles on stones round the water's edge, stepping stones out to the bench, dark reeds and three bare pale birches behind. Angus & Julia Stone: two chairs a little apart on an old rug facing the sea under a canopy of fairy lights, a crate between them with two mugs and a paper plane ("Big Jet Plane"), a guitar case open on the sand, and a silver coin shining in front of them. Tracy Chapman: an old car on the sand, headlights on into the mist. Israel Kamakawiwo'ole: a ukulele on a bench by a little pool, with a lei. Nick Drake: a rowing boat under a pink moon lantern. Everyone else, something left on the shore that fits their song: a beach hut boarded up for the season, wind chimes of shells and sea glass for Ryley Walker's "The West Wind", a striped windsock for unclepat, a silhouette on a dune, a ship's figurehead in the sand, two guitars on a log, seabirds on a rock, a raven on a post, three crates round an old radio, a lemonade stand, a lifebuoy, a lantern by a driftwood marker. The artists sit close round the lagoon, the three with their own radio on different sides of it, so the heart of the shore is full and the wilds are for wandering.

### The Bedroom Pop Meadows
*23 songs · 19 artists · 0.47 km² · built in Unity · Pyrosolstice's land*

**Arriving:** A deep lilac field just past sunset: the sun has gone, the sky is violet with a pink glow low down, and fireflies are coming out over the grass. In the middle lies a giant unmade bed, its duvet spilling down to the grass so you can walk up onto it, with huge pillows, a pair of giant headphones, and a bedside lamp that lights the whole field.

**The vibe:** The daydream half of bedroom music (the Small Hours is the other half: the making). This is the music you pace your room to for hours, so the land's spine is a track: a loop you can walk round and round, past every artist, lined both sides with fairy lights. A pulse of light chases round them, quicker on energetic songs, slower on quiet ones, with a dash of light at every new song. Quiet songs bring out more fireflies and fade glow-in-the-dark star stickers into the sky, like the ones on a bedroom ceiling; happy songs send soap bubbles up from the field and turn the light pinker. Deep lilac grass, purple clover, pastel flowers and dandelion clocks; trees hung with fairy lights. Bedroom things dreamed out into the field: glowing lava lamps, giant polaroids of the land's own covers, paper planes stuck nose-first in the grass, puffy star cushions, fairy lights on tall poles, and pastel paper lanterns rising slowly into the sky. A staircase of giant books leads up onto the bed.

**Artist areas:** Small daydream scenes off the loop, each in a soft version of the colour of whoever added it. Clairo: a giant swing hanging from a great tree full of fairy lights. beabadoobee: a blanket fort big enough to walk into, glowing inside. Everyone else: a hammock between trees, a beanbag and lamp, a heap of pillows, a swing, a paper-star mobile or a big plush bear. The older generation of the music (The Postal Service, fun., Foster the People, The Apples in Stereo) is faded, like an older dream.

### Garage Rock Row
*20 songs · 13 artists · 0.63 km²*

**Arriving:** A gritty small-town main street of garages, pubs and back alleys, ending in a neon casino.

**The vibe:** Late night. Muscle cars, pub signs, leather jackets on hooks.

**Artist areas:** One open garage per artist, each with a band set up inside. Arctic Monkeys get the one nearest the casino.

### The Electric Fields
*18 songs · 9 artists · 0.65 km²*

**Arriving:** Open festival fields on rolling hills. A huge festival tent with a disco ball bursting through its roof.

**The vibe:** Festival evening. Flags, string lights, synths on hay bales, giant inflatables.

**Artist areas:** MGMT gets a hall (the big tent). Others are smaller tents and stages.

### The Small Hours
*15 songs plus a whole discography · 11 artists · 0.66 km² · built in Unity*

**Arriving:** 3 am, deep indigo, a quiet drizzle. By the way in, an all-night laundrette glows, its machines turning. Wet stone lanes under orange street lamps lead up into a hollow of tiny homes, every window lit.

**The vibe:** The making half of bedroom music (the Bedroom Pop Meadows next door is the daydream). These are small, newer artists, and every home is somewhere music is being made right now: walk in and there's a desk under a lamp, a laptop, a microphone, plants, and their covers on the wall. Moss, ferns and garden hedges; stars through gaps in the cloud.

**Artist areas:** Steph.fm broadcasts from a radio cottage with an aerial and an ON AIR sign. Bell Thompson's room glows jazz-club blue, with a trumpet on the sill and a New York fire escape up the side. Boowah plays a pedal steel on the porch. runfromobm has a grilled cheese on the stove. Sōra has a streetlight outside; Fionavair's window flickers with the TV; Hailey Picardi's room is chaos; John Luther's is full of plants; Carmen Silver's lamp lights floating dust; Josie on the Rocks has a cat on the windowsill.

**Noah Gallagher's place:** apart from the hamlet, built with him. The cream bungalow with the screened porch where his first album was made, now his studio: his koa Languedoc on a stand, both album covers on the walls, a swing set, a birdbath and white geese in a yard of dry winter grass under huge bare trees. Behind it grows the night forest from the cover of Akashic Sanctuary, dark twisted trunks with teal light falling between them, purple and green orbs in the branches and a huge yellow moon with a magenta halo. A path winds through it past a light for each of that album's eight songs to a clearing with a still pool and the Ferris wheel from The Twelfth House: twelve gondolas, one for each sign and each of its twelve songs, round a yin-yang hub, while ribbons of aurora snake through the trees just over head height. His station covers the whole place and plays everything he has made; whichever song is playing, its light or its gondola glows.

## Artist areas: the concept

Every artist has a round plot already saved on the map. These are ideas for what goes on it.

### Four sizes, one recipe

| Tier | Songs | Plot | Artists | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shrine | 1 | ~30 m across | 976 | One object (a plinth, a bench, a sign, a jukebox) with a plaque and the album cover |
| Monument | 2–5 | ~36–44 m | 315 | A small built thing: a porch, a stall, a booth. Cover art for each song on show |
| Hall | 6–15 | ~90 m | 31 | A building you can walk into, with the songs on the walls |
| Temple | 16+ | ~140 m | 3 | A place of its own inside the land (Phish, The Beatles, Grateful Dead) |

Every tier has the same three parts, just bigger:
1. **A plaque:** name, number of songs, years (for example 1967–1975), top genres, who added the songs (by display name), and maybe one line from Last.fm.
2. **The covers:** each song's album cover, with its title. We already have the cover list from the original map.
3. **A marker:** a small light or flag you can see from a distance, so you know an artist is there.

### Each land dresses its artists in its own style

The plot, plaque and covers stay the same everywhere, but what holds them changes per land, from the "Artist areas" lines above: a cabin in Lanternwood, a container in the Docklands, a crystal shard in the Dubstep Shards, a statue in the Concert Isles. In practice that means about 8 style kits (one per realm), each with a few variations coloured by the land's palette. That way 66 lands are about 8 sets of models, not 66.

### Every land: light and living ground

Two things every land gets, each in its own way:
- **Bloom and plenty of light.** Lanterns, glowing markers, lit windows, halos and lights that answer the music. Glow colours stay strong enough to keep their colour once they bloom.
- **Its own ground cover that moves in the wind,** so the ground is never flat paint: the Indie Downs' long golden grass and poppies, the hub's kept lawn and wild golden meadow, Lanternwood's frost-tipped grass and rusty bracken poking up through the snow.

### Music in an artist area

- **Artists with 3+ songs have their own station.** Step onto the plot and you hear only that artist, on the same shared clock, so friends standing there together hear the same song. Step off and you're back on the land's station.
- **When the land's station plays a song, that artist's marker lights up.** So walking through a land, you can see whose song is on, and follow the light to find them.
- **Smaller shrines keep the land's station.** There are too many to give each its own, and it keeps the land's mix.

### Paths and plazas

- Each land has a plaza in the middle with its landmark.
- Paths should run from the plaza past the bigger artists and fade into open land. The shrines then feel like things you stumble on, not a grid.
- The spare plots stay empty land until new artists arrive. When the playlist grows, new shrines appear there without moving anyone.

### Open questions for you

Decided: **each land keeps its own sky** (no shared day and night), and **plaques show who added the songs**, by display names you choose (kept in the gitignored `build/display_names.json`, never the Spotify usernames).

2. **Featured artists** (like Skrillex on an Ellie Goulding song): a signpost pointing to the other artist's area, or ignore for now?
