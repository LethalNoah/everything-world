# The Everything World: a walking guide

What each place should feel like before anything is built in Unity. This is a design draft to react to: change anything that feels wrong. The hard facts (names, palettes, landmarks) live in `build/scene_art.json` and `ART_PLAN.md`; this guide is the vibe on top.

**How to read an entry:** what you see as you arrive, what it feels like to be there (light, weather, sound), and what the artist areas there should look like. Numbers are songs, artists and land area.

## The whole world in one paragraph

About 15.5 × 10.4 km. One big continent in the middle (the Old Kingdoms joined to Riffland), three landmasses around it (the Groovelands to the west, the Indie Isles to the east, the Chart Isles further east), and island chains toward the edge (the Boroughs top-left, the Bass Reaches bottom-right, the Isles of Story in an inner sea). In the middle of that inner sea sits the Everything Hub. Each realm has one fast-travel tower. Every land is at least 0.44 km², big enough to wander in for a few minutes without passing an artist area.

**Where the music changes, you can see it (the owner's idea).** Wherever two lands meet there's a shimmering curtain: invisible from far away, fading in as you approach, and always see-through. It's tinted with the colour of the land you're about to enter, so the same border looks different from each side. Bridges have one at each end. The areas of artists with their own radio station have a warm gold one round them, exactly where the music switches to that artist. Each kind has its own pattern (land borders a soft shimmer, bridge gates horizontal bands, artist rings vertical stripes), and as you walk up to one, the name of what's beyond fades in on it, so overlapping curtains stay easy to tell apart.

**Built for a group of friends.** Every building is sized for several people at once, not one: doorways at least 2.8 m wide and 3.4 m tall, rooms people gather in at least 8 m across, 3 m aisles, and places to sit. Lands should feel spread out, with space between things. (The numbers live in `HumanScale.cs`.)

**Light comes from things in the world.** Dusk and night lands keep their ambient light low, so lanterns, windows, fires and candles actually light the ground around them, each with a soft glow.

**One idea that runs through everything: each land keeps its own sky.** Stepping from the Hair Metal Strip (neon night) into the Yacht Rock Riviera (endless sunset) changes the light, fog and weather over a few seconds, the way the music changes. The world has no shared day and night; each place is always at its best time of day. (Decided 2026-11-08.)

## The Everything Hub

*The whole playlist, 2,166 songs · a round island in the inner sea · tower: The Everything Mast*

**Arriving:** A broad round island alone in the inner sea, always in golden late-afternoon light. Long causeways reach it from the Prog Marches, the Indie Downs and Studio Lot Island, and each one becomes a paved path into the plaza. Round the whole hub, a great ring of album covers turns slowly; you pass under it as you arrive.

**The vibe:** A grand crossroads, and a trophy hall for the whole playlist. This is what all of us built together, song by song, and the hub is proud of it. The station plays anything from the whole playlist, so you never know what's next. It's the place to meet before heading out and to come back to afterwards. Every visit is an occasion, whatever day it is.

**What's here:** No artists, on purpose. Instead:
- **The ring of every cover.** Every song's album cover, big (over 3 m), in the order it was added, spiralling up round the whole hub from the first song to the newest, with the year marked where each new one begins. The lowest turn starts just over head height, so you can walk up under the first songs and look at them closely. Each person who adds songs has their own bright frame colour (the dais tablet shows whose is whose). The song playing now gets a white frame and a beam of light down to the ground beneath it. The ring turns once every 50 minutes and can be seen from across the water.
- **The dais.** A round stone platform at the foot of the mast, ringed in glowing bronze, with ramps on four sides. Standing tablets carry the playlist's numbers and the names of everyone who built it.
- **The stands.** Curved stone tiers all round the plaza, facing the mast, for sitting together under the ring. They open where the paths come in.
- **The jukebox.** A classic arched jukebox with rainbow light tubes round its top, showing the song on the radio, its cover, its artist and which land it belongs to. Beside it, a big wooden arrow on a post swings round to point at that land, with the land's name and distance written on it.
- **The signpost.** One arm for every realm's tower, with the distance.
- **The map board.** The whole world's map on a big standing board, north up, with a glowing "you are here" dot.
- **The park.** Past the stands, a formal park: rows of round trees and clipped dark cones, planted in rings. Between the rows, an avenue of white stone statues of music notation on plinths: treble and bass clefs, notes, a sharp, a flat, a fermata, a rest.
- **The sky.** Always golden late afternoon, but alive: clouds drift on the day's wind (some hours clear, some hours full), gulls circle the mast, and the light takes its colour from each song, bright amber for happy songs and rose and violet for sad ones. Every new song sends a golden rocket up from the dais, and some songs (more often the more energetic they are) get a fireworks show over the ring.
- **The quiet bench.** At the water's edge, away from the paths and the stands: one bench, one lantern, no sign.

## The Old Kingdoms (centre of the continent)

*Classic rock, jam bands, prog, southern rock, glam and heavy metal · tower: The Old Radio Tower*

The heartland. Old hills, long roads and the tallest mountains in the world. Most of it feels like a timeless afternoon, with darker metal country in the high peaks.

### The Prog Marches
*37 songs · 13 artists · 0.54 km² · built in Unity*

**Arriving:** A wide, strange marchland under a total eclipse: low in the sky a black sun hangs in a blazing corona, the sky is deep violet overhead and amber at the horizon, and the stars are out. **The vibe:** Thoughtful and a little unsettling, and above all Pink Floyd's: they are the heart of the land (the Prism itself is theirs), and every Floyd song on the playlist is a place out in the marches, reached by its own trail, with a stone sign and a glow that wakes while its song plays. ## Artist areas: the concept

Every artist has a round plot already saved on the map. These are ideas for what goes on it.

### Four sizes, one recipe

| Tier | Songs | Plot | Artists | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shrine | 1 | ~30 m across | 976 | One object (a plinth, a bench, a sign, a jukebox) with a plaque and the album cover |
| Monument | 2–5 | ~36–44 m | 315 | A small built thing: a porch, a stall, a booth. Cover art for each song on show |
| Hall | 6–15 | ~90 m | 31 | A building you can walk into, with the songs on the walls |
| Temple | 16+ | ~140 m | 3 | A place of its own inside the land (Phish, The Beatles, Grateful Dead) |

Every tier has the same three parts, just bigger:
1. **A plaque:** name, number of songs, years (for example 1967–1975), top genres, who added the songs (by display name), and maybe one line from Last.fm.
2. **The covers:** each song's album cover, with its title. We already have the cover list from the original map.
3. **A marker:** a small light or flag you can see from a distance, so you know an artist is there.

### Each land dresses its artists in its own style

The plot, plaque and covers stay the same everywhere, but what holds them changes per land, from the "Artist areas" lines above: a cabin in Lanternwood, a container in the Docklands, a crystal shard in the Dubstep Shards, a statue in the Concert Isles. In practice that means about 8 style kits (one per realm), each with a few variations coloured by the land's palette. That way 66 lands are about 8 sets of models, not 66.

### Every land: light and living ground

Two things every land gets, each in its own way:
- **Bloom and plenty of light.** Lanterns, glowing markers, lit windows, halos and lights that answer the music. Glow colours stay strong enough to keep their colour once they bloom.
- **Its own ground cover that moves in the wind,** so the ground is never flat paint: the Indie Downs' long golden grass and poppies, the hub's kept lawn and wild golden meadow, Lanternwood's frost-tipped grass and rusty bracken poking up through the snow.

### Music in an artist area

- **Artists with 3+ songs have their own station.** Step onto the plot and you hear only that artist, on the same shared clock, so friends standing there together hear the same song. Step off and you're back on the land's station.
- **When the land's station plays a song, that artist's marker lights up.** So walking through a land, you can see whose song is on, and follow the light to find them.
- **Smaller shrines keep the land's station.** There are too many to give each its own, and it keeps the land's mix.

### Paths and plazas

- Each land has a plaza in the middle with its landmark.
- Paths should run from the plaza past the bigger artists and fade into open land. The shrines then feel like things you stumble on, not a grid.
- The spare plots stay empty land until new artists arrive. When the playlist grows, new shrines appear there without moving anyone.
